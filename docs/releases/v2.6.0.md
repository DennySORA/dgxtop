**dgxtop** is a terminal GPU monitor built for NVIDIA DGX Spark: every GPU, each job's share of its memory and the whole machine on one screen, plus a record of every GPU job from start to end. It runs on any Linux machine with NVIDIA GPUs.

## New in v2.6.0

- **A slowed GPU says so.** When the driver holds a GPU's clocks down for heat, a hardware signal or a power cap, a banner on every view names the GPU, the reason and the clock it runs at against its maximum (`GPU 0 slowed down by its driver: thermal slowdown · clock 1976 of 3003 MHz, 34% below its maximum`), and the GPU's line shows `▼` with the reason beside its temperature. The banner stays ten seconds past the last such reading, so it does not flicker.
- **Memory pressure.** The kernel's stall pressure (PSI) of memory, CPU and I/O over 10 s, 1 min and 5 min: the share of time work stood still waiting for that resource. On a DGX Spark's unified memory it answers whether a slowdown came from memory, where a VRAM figure would be made up. With it: what moves in and out of swap each second, and how many processes the OOM killer has killed since boot. The Overview History charts the memory's pressure and the CPU's and the SoC's temperature.
- **The memory panel in rows.** One subject a row, each led by its label in its own color: `RAM`, `GPU`, `used`, `avail`, `swap`. `avail` and `swap` carry their share of the whole, `avail`'s in the color of how little is left. A pressure has a line only while one of its averages is above zero.
- **More of the CPU.** The line shows the limit the cpufreq policy sets where it holds the cores below their maximum (`2.9/3.0 (3.9) GHz`), names the hottest cluster on a DGX Spark (`81°C cluster 1p`), and writes each load average in the color of that many CPUs busy.
- **More of each process.** `USER` is the login name, and a process that encodes or decodes video shows its share of the GPU's encoder and decoder (`ENC`, `DEC`).
- **A snapshot for scripts.** `dgxtop --snapshot-json` prints every current metric and the GPU processes as one JSON document and exits; it runs beside a running dgxtop and keeps nothing. `--redact` takes out the host's name, GPU UUIDs and serial numbers, paths, interface names and the processes' and users' names, so the document can be pasted into an issue.

## Changed

- In the Overview History, `s` hides the memory panel's two pressure strips. A configuration that has no folds of its own starts with those strips, the temperature panel and the power panel folded; `s`, `t`, `p` or `o` bring them back, and unfolding everything is now remembered.
- The memory panel's GPU legend leads with `GPU` in place of `[GPU]`.
- A configuration saved by this version with the pressure strips folded is refused by an older dgxtop.

## Fixed

- The memory panel showed an `other` entry for a second or two whenever a GPU job's memory grew, on a line of its own when the legend was full, so the panel kept changing its height. What the other GPU processes hold is now counted from the same process list as the jobs' own figures.
- `--replay` showed no GPU process on a host that had booted less than a quarter of an hour ago.

## Install

```bash
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DennySORA/dgxtop/main/install.sh | bash
```

Linux on x86_64 or aarch64 with glibc 2.34 or newer (Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12, RHEL 9 and later), DGX Spark included; WSL 2 runs the Linux build. The installer checks the archive against `SHA256SUMS` and keeps the previous version for `./install.sh --rollback`. `dgxtop-v2.6.0.cdx.json` lists the dependencies (CycloneDX SBOM).
